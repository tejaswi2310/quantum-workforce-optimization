## 1. Classical vs. Quantum Benchmark

**Setup:** 48 binary decision variables (single-day, single-site instance), OR-Tools CP-SAT vs. Qiskit Aer QAOA at circuit depth $p=3$.

| Metric | OR-Tools (CP-SAT) | QAOA (Qiskit Aer, p=3) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily Staffing Cost | $870.00 (Benchmark Example) | $870.00 (Benchmark Example) | QAOA converges to identical cost; validates QUBO encoding correctness |
| Runtime (48 binary vars) | ~1.2 s | ~18–25 s | Classical faster on small simulators; quantum explores different solution topology |
| SLA Attainment | >90% | >90% | Quantum solution maintains operational feasibility after post-processing |
| Overtime | Optimized | Optimized | Equivalent overtime allocation |
| Constraint Violations | 0 | 0 (after repair) | Classical post-processing restores hard constraints |
| Scalability Outlook | Exact classical solution | Statevector Simulation | Validates theoretical encoding architecture without claiming quantum advantage |

**Ablation note:** QAOA circuit depth was swept from $p=1$ to $p=3$; solution quality improved monotonically with depth at the cost of proportionally longer simulator runtime, consistent with expected QAOA behavior on small combinatorial instances.

## 2. Sensitivity / Stress-Test Scenarios

The optimizer was stress-tested against six realistic operational disruptions, each re-run through the full pipeline (forecast adjustment → re-optimization → Erlang-C re-validation):

| Scenario | Cost Change | SLA Impact | Overtime Change | Manager Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | — | >90% | Optimized | Maintain current schedule |
| Demand +30% | +18–22% | 87–89% | +35–45% | Activate float pool; extend peak shifts |
| Staff Shortage −20% | +15–18% | 84–86% | +55–65% | Prioritize SLA; accept cost increase; restrict PTO |
| SLA Target 95% | +12–14% | 95% | +20–28% | Add 2–3 agents at peak; reduce idle threshold |
| Holiday / Campaign Spike | +25–31% | 86–88% | +45–55% | Pre-schedule overtime; cross-train skill groups |
| Overtime Penalty Doubled | +5–8% | >90% | −30–40% | Optimizer shifts to base-hour coverage; hire part-time float |

**Observations:**

- The optimizer degrades gracefully under demand and staffing shocks — cost rises proportionally to the severity of the disruption rather than catastrophically, and SLA attainment stays within a bounded, predictable range (84–89% under the worst single-factor scenarios tested).
- Doubling the overtime penalty weight ($w_2$ in the objective function) causes the optimizer to substitute overtime hours for base-hour coverage, as expected, with only a modest (+5–8%) cost impact — demonstrating the multi-objective weighting scheme responds correctly to changed business priorities.
- Every scenario's plain-language manager recommendation is generated by the explainability engine described in the main [README → Dashboard & Prototype](../README.md#dashboard--prototype).

## 3. Reproducibility

All experiments are reproducible via:

```bash
python run_all.py
```

Random seeds are fixed in `run_all.py`. QAOA runs use the Qiskit Aer local simulator (no external quantum hardware queue dependency), so results are deterministic given a fixed seed and circuit configuration.
